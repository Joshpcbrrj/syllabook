# Profile: knowledge (history, sciences, concepts, reading topics)

## Topic order

guiding question → context (when, where, who) → the story or explanation → people and discoveries → sources and evidence → interpretations or debates → synthesis (answer to the guiding question).

## Practice

- Comprehension questions that require explaining, not repeating ("why did the transistor make computers smaller and cheaper?").
- Ordering events on a timeline; matching causes and effects.
- Analyzing a source: who wrote it, when, for whom, what it shows and what it leaves out.
- Comparing two interpretations.
- Answers hidden and commented; open answers with a short rubric.

## Verification

- Every date, number, name and quotation goes through `facts.py` and the sources (`SOURCES.md` §5).
- **Fact × interpretation × legend** are separated in the text. Popular stories are told as stories and then checked against the records.
- **Curiosities only with a source**; label them as curiosities.

## Visuals

Timelines (`timeline`), concept trees with verbs in the labels and cause-and-effect trees (`tree`), real period images with credits (photos, documents, maps of the time). A free-form concept map or a drawn geographic map is not supported by `diagram.py`: use a tree, a table or a real map with a free license.

## Cautions

- Show the people often left out of older narratives when the sources support it.
- Sensitive topics: neutral wording, more than one perspective when historians disagree, and no judgment presented as fact.
